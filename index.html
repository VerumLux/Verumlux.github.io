Why this stays yours

The helper works for your shop, in accounts you own, under rules you set. You can pause it. You can walk away. Your data stays with you.

You own your accounts and data

Sign-ins stay in your name. The helper is a guest you can invite, limit, and revoke. Your lists, mail, and books remain yours.

The tools you already use

We start with the email, calendar, and books you already have — not a stack you have to adopt to get help.

Sensitive work can stay put

When a job should not leave the building, it can run on a computer you own. Only a de-identified note needs to come back, if anything does.

No lock-in

You can pause the helper or stop altogether. Your accounts, files, and customer relationships stay with you.

And the rule that does not move: nothing goes out in your name without your approval.

A practice example

Illustrative example — not a real shop, and not a measured result

Lantern & Twine

Lantern & Twine is a fictional gift-wrap and monogram kiosk, imagined inside an Exchange. In the sketch, the work that followed the owner home was pickup reminders and after-hours “is it ready?” texts. Wrapping and monogramming stayed in human hands.

The practice design was a helper that would draft those reminders for a short morning approval. On paper, that is about two hours a week back on pickup reminders. It is a teaching example of the method — not a client story, and not a promise of the same hours in another shop.

You stay in charge

Nothing goes out in your name without your approval. Your data stays yours. You choose what the helper can see, and you can pause it whenever you want.
